#+TITLE: F0 overlay
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:t

Implementation: [[file:../../spiny/overlays/f0/extractor.py]].

The normalized autocorrelation of P. Boersma (1993), the method of Praat.

* Candidates

#+begin_example
window = Hanning of 3 periods of the lowest F0
a(τ)   = autocorrelation(frame × window) / autocorrelation(window)       by FFT, normalized at τ = 0
#+end_example

Dividing by the autocorrelation of the window removes the decay due to the window: a periodic signal gets close to 1 at
its period. Only the band up to 4 times the highest F0 is kept before the inverse FFT, which makes it much smaller, and a
parabola through the three points around each peak gives the period precisely.

Each frame keeps its 6 best peaks, and one candidate "unvoiced":

#+begin_example
voiced strength   = height − 0.01 × log2(lowest F0 × period)
unvoiced strength = voicing threshold + max(2 − relative peak × (1 + voicing threshold) / silence threshold, 0)
#+end_example

* Path search

A Viterbi search finds the best path through the candidates. A jump costs 0.35 per octave and a change between voiced and
unvoiced 0.14, for a 10 ms step (these costs are scaled by 10 ms / time step, as more frames mean less per frame). Choosing
the best peak of each frame alone gives many more octave errors.

To join the chunks, each one is computed with 25 more frames on each side.

| Parameter         | Default |
|-------------------+---------|
| Time step         | 5 ms    |
| Minimum F0        | 75 Hz   |
| Maximum F0        | 500 Hz  |
| Voicing threshold | 0.45    |
| Silence threshold | 0.03    |

The median difference with Praat is 0.14 Hz on the voiced frames of the example recordings, with gross errors on at most
0.2 % of them.
